ABSTRACT
This research focuses on improving driver behavior analysis by addressing the limitations of
existing generalized driver monitoring systems. Traditional approaches often rely on fixed
thresholds and uniform models, which fail to consider the unique driving patterns of individual
drivers, leading to inaccurate detection of risky behavior.
To overcome this limitation, this study proposes a personalized driver behavioral deviation
detection approach that models individual driving characteristics and identifies deviations in real
time. The system utilizes key driving parameters such as speed, acceleration, braking patterns,
and steering behavior to establish a baseline profile for each driver. Real-time data is
continuously compared against this personalized baseline to detect abnormal behavior.
The proposed methodology includes data collection, preprocessing, personalized behavior
modeling, and deviation detection using threshold-based and pattern-based analysis techniques.
The system is capable of classifying detected deviations into different risk levels, enabling more
meaningful interpretation of driver behavior.
Experimental results demonstrate that the personalized approach improves the accuracy and
reliability of deviation detection compared to traditional generalized systems. The system
effectively distinguishes between normal and abnormal driving patterns while reducing false
positive detections.
The findings highlight the importance of incorporating personalization into driver monitoring
systems and demonstrate the potential of the proposed approach in enhancing road safety and
intelligent transportation applications.

INTRODUCTION
Background and Literature Overview
Road traffic accidents remain one of the leading causes of injuries and fatalities worldwide, with
human error identified as a primary contributing factor. Among these, unsafe driving behaviors
such as harsh acceleration, sudden braking, and erratic steering play a significant role in
increasing accident risks. As a result, there has been a growing interest in developing intelligent
systems capable of monitoring driver behavior and predicting potential risks in real time.
Traditional driver monitoring systems mainly focus on generalized behavior analysis using
predefined thresholds or rule-based mechanisms. These systems typically evaluate parameters
such as speed, acceleration, and braking patterns to detect abnormal driving behavior. While such
approaches provide basic insights, they fail to account for individual differences in driving styles.
For instance, a behavior that may be considered aggressive for one driver could be normal for
another, leading to inaccurate assessments.
Recent advancements in intelligent transportation systems and data-driven approaches have
introduced more sophisticated methods for analyzing driving behavior. Machine learning and
data analytics techniques have been widely used to identify patterns in driver behavior and detect
anomalies. These approaches utilize large datasets to model driving patterns and classify
behaviors into categories such as safe or risky. However, many of these systems rely on
generalized models trained on aggregated data, which limits their ability to adapt to individual
drivers.
Furthermore, existing research in driver monitoring often focuses on specific aspects such as
drowsiness detection, fatigue analysis, or road condition monitoring. While these components are
important, there is comparatively less emphasis on personalized behavioral deviation
detection, where the system learns and adapts to the unique driving characteristics of each
individual.
Therefore, there is a need for a more adaptive and personalized approach that can accurately
distinguish between normal and abnormal driving behaviors based on individual driving patterns.

--- PAGE 9 ---
Research Gap
Despite the availability of various driver monitoring and risk prediction systems, several
limitations can be identified in existing approaches.
Firstly, most existing systems rely on generalized models that apply uniform thresholds or
classification rules across all drivers. These models do not consider individual variations in
driving behavior, which can lead to false positives or inaccurate risk detection. For example, a
driver who naturally exhibits higher acceleration patterns may be incorrectly classified as risky
under a generalized system.
Secondly, current systems often focus on isolated behavioral parameters rather than analyzing
comprehensive driving patterns over time. This limits the ability to detect subtle behavioral
deviations that may indicate potential risks.
Additionally, many research efforts prioritize specific components such as driver drowsiness
detection or environmental monitoring, while neglecting the importance of continuous
behavioral deviation analysis. As a result, the integration of personalized behavioral modeling
into real-time monitoring systems remains limited.
Most importantly, there is a lack of systems that dynamically adapt to individual drivers by
establishing personalized baseline profiles and detecting deviations relative to those baselines.
This absence of personalization reduces the effectiveness of existing solutions in real-world
scenarios.
Therefore, the key research gap identified in this study is:
The lack of a personalized driver behavioral deviation detection approach that can model
individual driving patterns and accurately identify deviations in real time.

--- PAGE 10 ---
Research Problem
Based on the identified research gap, the main problem addressed in this study is the inability of
existing driver monitoring systems to accurately detect risky driving behavior due to the absence
of personalization.
Current systems fail to differentiate between normal and abnormal behavior for individual
drivers, as they rely on generalized assumptions. This leads to unreliable detection results and
limits the practical applicability of such systems in real-world environments.
Furthermore, the absence of adaptive mechanisms to learn from individual driving patterns
prevents these systems from improving over time. As driving behavior is highly subjective and
varies across individuals, a one-size-fits-all approach is insufficient for accurate risk prediction.
Therefore, there is a need to develop a system that can:
• Learn and model individual driving behavior
• Continuously monitor real-time driving data
• Detect deviations relative to personalized baselines
• Provide meaningful insights into potential risks
Research Objectives
Main Objective
The main objective of this research is to develop a personalized driver behavioral deviation
detection system that can model individual driving patterns and identify deviations in real time to
support risk prediction.

METHODOLOGY
Overview of the Approach
The proposed system focuses on detecting deviations in driver behavior by establishing a
personalized baseline for each driver and continuously monitoring real-time driving patterns.
Unlike traditional driver monitoring systems that rely on generalized thresholds, this approach
emphasizes individualized behavioral modeling to improve accuracy and reliability.
The overall workflow of the system consists of several key stages: data collection, preprocessing,
baseline behavior modeling, deviation detection, and result generation. Initially, driving-related
data such as speed, acceleration, braking intensity, and steering patterns are collected. This data
is then processed to identify normal behavioral patterns unique to each driver.
Once a baseline profile is established, incoming real-time data is continuously compared against
this personalized model. Any significant deviation from the baseline is identified as a potential
risk indicator. The system then generates alerts or classifications based on the severity of the
deviation.
This approach enables a more adaptive and context-aware driver monitoring system, capable of
distinguishing between normal variations in driving style and actual risky behavior.
Data Collection
The effectiveness of the proposed system heavily depends on the quality and relevance of the
collected data. The system utilizes driving-related parameters that directly reflect driver behavior.
Data Types
The following types of data are considered:
• Speed-related data (vehicle speed over time)
• Acceleration and deceleration patterns
• Braking intensity and frequency
• Steering wheel movements
• Time-based driving patterns
These attributes were selected because they provide meaningful insights into driver behavior and
can be used to identify abnormal patterns.

Data can be obtained through:

Personalized Behavior Modeling
A key contribution of this research is the development of a personalized behavioral model for
each driver.
Instead of applying fixed thresholds across all users, the system builds a unique baseline profile
using historical driving data. This baseline represents the normal driving behavior of an
individual, capturing their typical speed ranges, acceleration habits, braking tendencies, and
steering patterns.
The modeling process involves:
• Collecting initial driving data over a defined period
• Extracting relevant features from the data
• Establishing average values and acceptable variation ranges
• Defining behavioral boundaries specific to the driver
This personalized approach ensures that the system adapts to different driving styles, reducing
false positives and improving detection accuracy.
Deviation Detection Mechanism
Once the baseline behavior is established, the system detects deviations by comparing real-time
driving data with the personalized model.
Deviation detection is performed using threshold-based and pattern-based analysis techniques.

--- PAGE 14 ---
Threshold-Based Detection
Each behavioral parameter is assigned a dynamic threshold derived from the baseline profile. If
real-time values exceed these thresholds, the system flags them as deviations.
For example:
• Sudden spikes in acceleration beyond normal range
• Harsh braking events
• Irregular steering patterns
Pattern-Based Analysis
In addition to threshold checks, the system analyzes behavioral trends over time. Repeated
abnormal patterns are treated as stronger indicators of risky driving.
This includes:
• Frequent rapid acceleration and braking cycles
• Consistent deviation in steering stability
• Irregular driving behavior over short intervals
Deviation Classification
Detected deviations are categorized based on severity levels such as:
• Low-risk deviation
• Moderate-risk deviation
• High-risk deviation
This classification helps in generating meaningful insights and alerts.

Conclusion
This research focused on addressing the limitations of existing driver monitoring systems by
introducing a personalized approach to behavioral deviation detection. Traditional systems often
rely on generalized thresholds and predefined rules, which fail to account for the unique driving
patterns of individual drivers. As a result, such systems may produce inaccurate or misleading
results when applied in real-world scenarios.
To overcome these limitations, this study proposed a personalized driver behavioral deviation
detection system that models individual driving behavior and identifies deviations in real time.
The system was designed to collect driving-related data, establish a baseline profile for each
driver, and continuously compare real-time behavior against this personalized model.
The results of the study demonstrate that the proposed approach is capable of effectively
distinguishing between normal and abnormal driving behavior. By adapting to individual driving
styles, the system reduces false detections and provides more meaningful insights into potential
risks. The ability to classify deviations based on severity further enhances the usefulness of the
system in practical applications.
